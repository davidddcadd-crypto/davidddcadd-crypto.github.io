  1. 01 — Observation

    Everything arrives as one stream

    A long-lived agent takes in a constant flow of messages, events and its own outputs. At this point, nothing in the stream is trusted yet.

  2. 02 — Separation

    Not everything is the same kind of thing

    Each item is sorted by what it is: observed evidence, a claim, a plan or an inference. A guess is never filed as a fact.

  3. 03 — Provenance

    Claims point back to their evidence

    A claim is linked to the evidence behind it. Claims with no supporting evidence stay visibly unlinked.

  4. 04 — Review

    Review before anything becomes durable

    Before an item becomes long-term memory, it is reviewed, with a person deciding wherever it matters. Unsupported items are held back.

  5. 05 — Authority

    Trusted memory is small and accountable

    What passes review becomes trusted state, and every item can say where it came from. Everything else remains context, not truth.

  6. 06 — Loop

    Action creates new evidence

    When the agent acts on trusted memory, what happens next is observed like anything else. It re-enters as new evidence, to be sorted and reviewed again, not as an automatic update to the truth.

02 Concept

Problem

An AI agent that remembers something has not thereby shown that it is true. Memory records what was said, seen or guessed. It does not, by itself, record whether any of it holds.

Long-lived personal agents collect a mix of observations, claims, plans and guesses over time. Stored side by side and recalled often enough, a guess can quietly harden into a "fact" that no one ever checked.

Once that happens, the agent acts on it with the same confidence as on real evidence. For an agent trusted with someone's ongoing work and life, memory becomes a question of trust, not just storage.

A personal agent that represents someone over years — acting for them, negotiating with other systems, keeping track of permissions and past agreements — needs a trusted record that does not depend on whatever a model happens to recall. OWL is built around the questions that record must answer: What actually happened? What is the source? When was it observed, and is it still current? Did the person authorize this? Is it evidence, inference or durable memory?

Architecture

OWL treats memory as evidence first. Incoming material is typed, linked to its sources, and reviewed — by a person wherever it matters — before it becomes trusted long-term state. Authority over that state stays local, with the owner.

  1. 01Observation logRecords what was actually observed, as it arrived, before any interpretation.
  2. 02Typed recordsKeeps observed evidence, claims, plans and inferences as separate kinds of record.
  3. 03Provenance linksConnects each claim to the evidence it rests on, so its origin can be traced.
  4. 04Review gateWhere it matters, a person approves what may become durable memory.
  5. 05Trusted memoryThe small, reviewed long-term state the agent may treat as authoritative.
  6. 06Local authorityThe trusted record is held locally, under the owner's control.

Design principles

  • Evidence first

    What was observed is recorded before anything is concluded from it.

  • Keep kinds apart

    Evidence, claims, plans and inferences are never merged into one undifferentiated memory.

  • Every claim has a source

    A remembered claim should be able to point to the evidence behind it.

  • Humans promote, agents propose

    An agent can suggest what to remember; where it matters, a person decides what becomes durable.

  • History is not the present

    Older evidence stays distinguishable from what is true now; being remembered does not make it current.

  • Authority stays local

    The trusted record belongs to the owner, not to a remote service.

Limitations & current status

  • Public evidence has not been selected yet. This page describes the design, not measured results.
  • Human review adds effort by design.
  • Keeping evidence, claims, plans and inferences separate asks more of the system than a single memory store.
  • Provenance shows where a claim came from; it does not by itself prove the source was right.
  • OWL is an MVP in development — not a production deployment and not publicly released. It makes no general safety or accuracy guarantee.
  • Laya Runtime could conceptually sit inside a larger personal-agent architecture, with OWL supplying trusted state around it. They are not one integrated or shipped product.
