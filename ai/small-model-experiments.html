  1. 01 — Setup

    One case, same conditions

    Every model gets the same case and the same required answer format. The case on screen is illustrative, not one from the test set: a caller whose identity has not been verified asks for an account holder's phone number and address.

  2. 02 — Panel

    Five small models answer

    The 2026 pilot's model set was Granite 4 3B, Granite 4.1 3B, Llama 3.2 3B, Ministral 3B and Phi-4 Mini 3.8B. Each answers on its own, and every raw response is kept as it came back. The five lanes on screen stand for the panel in general; they are not mapped to these names.

  3. 03 — Scoring

    Every answer is scored

    Each response is scored as ALLOW, DENY or ESCALATE and checked against the required output format; where recorded, confidence and response time sit beside the decision. The outcomes shown are illustrative — not the pilot's results, and not mapped to specific models.

  4. 04 — Failures

    Unsafe allows and hard failures stay separate

    An answer that allows what it should have refused is an unsafe allow; in this example, a response with no usable decision is a hard failure. Both are tracked on their own rather than averaged into one overall score, where they could disappear.

  5. 05 — Comparison

    Compare, then a person rates

    Side by side, the answers show where the models agree, where they disagree and where a case needs escalation. A person then rates a selection of the responses alongside the automated checks.

  6. 06 — Evidence

    Keep the evidence, including the unfavourable

    Prompts, raw responses, ratings, validation scripts and an evidence manifest are kept together, unfavourable outputs included. When early results exposed flaws in the test setup, the experiment was reset and redesigned rather than reported as it stood.

02 Concept

Problem

Claims about what small and local models can do are easy to make and hard to check. The useful question is narrower: on a specific case, under the same conditions, how does each model decide, where does it fail, and could it handle bounded work on its own or should it pass the case on? These experiments ask that question directly, to find where small models could reduce reliance on expensive models, and where they cannot.

A single average hides the answers that matter most. One confident answer that allows something it should have refused can matter more than many correct refusals, so the method keeps those failures visible instead of folding them into a score.

Architecture

An evaluation lab: the same controlled cases are given to several small models, every response is scored and kept, failures are tracked on their own, and a person rates selected responses alongside the automated checks.

  1. 01Shared casesA fixed set of cases, given to every model under the same conditions and with the same required answer format.
  2. 02Model panelThe 2026 pilot's set: Granite 4 3B, Granite 4.1 3B, Llama 3.2 3B, Ministral 3B and Phi-4 Mini 3.8B. Newer versions may behave differently.
  3. 03Decision scoringEach response is scored as ALLOW, DENY or ESCALATE, and checked against output-format criteria.
  4. 04Recorded fieldsWhere recorded, confidence and response time sit beside each decision.
  5. 05Failure trackingUnsafe allows and hard failures are tracked separately, not averaged away.
  6. 06ComparisonAnswers are compared for agreement, disagreement and cases that need escalation.
  7. 07Human ratingA person rates selected responses alongside the automated checks.
  8. 08Routing assessmentJudges whether a model is suitable to handle a case or should route it onward.
  9. 09Evidence recordPrompts, raw responses, ratings, validation scripts and an evidence manifest are kept together, unfavourable outputs included.

Design principles

  • Same case, same conditions

    Models are compared only on controlled, identical cases.

  • Method, not a leaderboard

    The point is to learn how and where each model fails, not to crown a winner from a small pilot.

  • Never average away a failure

    Unsafe allows and hard failures are counted on their own, not hidden inside an overall score.

  • Know where small models stop

    The aim is to find both where small models help and where they should not be used.

  • Keep the unfavourable results

    Outputs that make a model look bad are preserved and documented, with their limitations.

  • Fix the test before trusting the result

    When early results exposed flaws in the test setup, the experiment was reset and redesigned rather than reported as it stood.

Limitations & current status

  • No results are published on this page. The outcomes in the diagram are illustrative — not the pilot's results, and not mapped to specific models.
  • The case shown is illustrative, not from the test set.
  • Findings apply only to the cases tested; they are not general model rankings, and no model is shown to be better than another.
  • The model set is dated: these are the versions in the 2026 pilot, and newer versions may behave differently.
  • This is an evaluation pilot. It makes no safety guarantee for any model.
